\section{The same build on a third machine: an M5 Max Mac Studio}\label{sec:studio}

On 2026-11-14 the work moved to a third machine, a Mac Studio with an Apple M5 Max, and on 2026-11-15 the build of
Section~\ref{sec:cg33} was measured on it, with the same library files as on the other two machines, not rebuilt:
cg33 is the fork's commit 13350ca (patches 0001--0043, the head; the CPU engine \code{multithreaded} and the GPU engine
\code{metal-diamond}), cg31 is ff2538a (patches 0001--0039) and stock is v0.37.0
(\code{results/studio\_speed\_set\_2026-11-15.txt}; the scripts are in \code{scripts/studio/}). These numbers form a
third set. They are compared only with each other: no table of this section holds a number of the M5 MacBook Air or of
the M5 Pro Mac mini, and no ratio between machines is given, since the chips differ in cores, caches, memory and
operating-system build at once. The M5 Pro Mac mini of Section~\ref{sec:m5pro} is no longer available, so its set is
closed as it stands.

\paragraph{The machine.} Mac Studio, Apple M5 Max (Mac17,14): 6 ``Super'' cores (\code{hw.perflevel0}, one L2 cache of
16\,MiB) and 12 ``Performance'' cores (\code{hw.perflevel1}, L2 of 8\,MiB), a 32-core GPU, 36\,GB of unified
memory, macOS~27.0 (26A425). With 18 cores outside the efficiency class, the automatic thread search of patch 0041
(Section~\ref{sec:cg33}) starts at 18 threads here; cg31's rule starts at the 6 Super cores.

\paragraph{Correctness on this chip.} On 2026-11-14, on a loaded machine (verdicts only), the command line was rebuilt
from a clean folder and its 260 tests in 28 suites passed, the 14 smoke tests included (so the eleven smoke tests that
start simulations, passed again). For the then-installed build cg31
and for cg33, the Debye check passed, the six models were identical to stock (the reduced check, 18 of 18 rows each),
the validation suite passed 15 of 15 and the Metal diamond check 72 of 72; the validation suite on the Metal engine
passed 15 of 15, and both boards ran end to end through the toolkit with the stop steps of the other machines (34\,300 and
50\,320). At 13:38 that day cg33 was installed as the author's working build (version string v0.37.0-61-g13350ca; the
library's sha-256 begins b81e39a0); the same checks were run again from the install and all passed. In the
measurements below every CPU run gave probes identical to its reference run, and the Metal rows differ from theirs by at
most $2.2\times10^{-7}$ relative (the CPU reference keeps subnormal numbers, the GPU flushes them).

\paragraph{How it was measured.} In one slot of a quiet block on the machine, 2026-11-15 from 06:49 to 08:23 (94
minutes), with no other measured job beside it: \code{scripts/studio/studioset.zsh} ran stages C (the speed table), S
(stock at eight thread counts), A (bandwidth, kernels and the thread sweep), E (the two boards end to end), B (the tiled
sweep) and D (the valley and the switches), in that order, and every stage exited 0. The rates are medians of interleaved
rounds of \code{bench/abtest.py} (4 rounds for the speed table, 3 for stock and for the thread sweep). Before each run the gate
(\code{scripts/studio/common.zsh}) waits up to 20\,s for the other processes to use at most 300\,\% CPU, 3 of the 18 cores,
and a run made above that is run once more; the record lists, for every row, the other processes' median CPU and GPU use during
its runs. The other processes used 0.9 to 1.6 cores during the speed table and during the patch tutorial's stock rows,
and 0.2 to 0.5 cores in the other stages. The end-to-end runs went through \code{emskit fdtd e2e}, one run each, beside 0.2
to 0.3 cores.

\paragraph{The thread search.} The automatic count of cg33 picked 18 threads in every round on all three models, which
gives 4\,756\,\MC\ (GCPW line), 6\,170 (notch x3) and 4\,801 (patch x5). Paired with 18 fixed threads in the speed rounds
(Table~\ref{tab:studiospeed}), the search of cg33 runs at 0.94 to 0.98$\times$ the fixed rate on five models and 1.04$\times$
on the patch tutorial, where 18 threads is not the best count; the rule of cg31 (start at the 6 Super cores and climb) ran at
0.70 to 0.86$\times$ on those five and 1.14$\times$ on the patch tutorial, and cg33 with \code{OPENEMS\_TUNE\_START=p}, which
restores that rule, 0.76$\times$ on notch x3 (4\,798 against 6\,302\,\MC). The new start therefore takes back most of the cost of the
search on this chip, as on the M5 Pro (Table~\ref{tab:cg33tune}). On patch x5 the search settles on 18 threads, the top of the
recovery after the dip, and misses the peak at 10 threads: its 4\,801\,\MC\ is 0.94$\times$ the peak of 5\,082 (the fixed count
of 18 threads is 4\,868, 4\,\% below it).

\subsection*{Memory bandwidth and the standalone kernels}
STREAM~\cite{stream} (version 5.10, 100\,M elements per array, OpenMP) gives a Triad rate of 132\,GB/s on one thread and 324,
286 and 321\,GB/s at 6, 12 and 18 (Table~\ref{tab:studioband}): the six Super cores alone reach the rate of all 18
threads, and the Triad rate at 12 threads is lower than at 6. The standalone fused kernel (\code{scripts/kernel/kb\_idx.cpp}
with \code{KB\_FUSED=1}, on a grid of the patch x5 size) runs 2\,879--3\,072\,\MC\ at 6 threads, 3\,180--3\,300 at 12 and 2\,020--2\,199 at
18; that drop at 18 threads was not investigated, and the engine's own fused sweep does not show it (Table~\ref{tab:studiothreads}).
With one independent sub-grid per thread (\code{kb\_tile.cpp}) the kernel runs at 3\,444, 5\,186 and 5\,391\,\MC\ at 6, 12 and 18
threads, 264\,GB/s at 18 threads at the 49\,B per cell of the traffic model (a computed figure, 82\,\% of the Triad rate at 18
threads). The same program's shared tile, in which all threads work on one tile for several steps, is 1.21$\times$ its fused sweep at 6
threads, 0.99$\times$ at 12 and 1.15$\times$ at 18.

\subsection*{The two boards end to end}
Table~\ref{tab:studioe2e} has the two boards end to end, as in Table~\ref{tab:e2e} and Table~\ref{tab:m5proe2e}, on this machine with
the toolkit's defaults (the engine picks the threads, the probe end at $-60$\,dB with a hold of 0.5, on the SMA launch the 0.05\,mm
mesh floor; \code{emskit fdtd e2e}, one run each, the other processes on 0.2 to 0.3 cores). Stock has no probe-end
criterion, so its runs go to \code{max\_time}, and the toolkit gave it its fallback thread counts, 4 for the GCPW line and 8 for the
SMA launch (the GCPW count is stock's best of Table~\ref{tab:studiostock}; the SMA launch's best of the counts measured there is 6).
The factors of 30 to 47 therefore have three parts: the probe end stops these runs after 2.7$\times$ (GCPW line) and 2.4$\times$ (SMA
launch) fewer steps (the mesh is the same in the stock run and the fork's, on the SMA launch the one with the 0.05\,mm floor); the faster
engine supplies the rest (the cell rate printed by the toolkit is 15.9$\times$ stock's on the GCPW line and 13.3$\times$ on the
SMA launch, 4\,798 and 5\,256 against 301 and 395\,\MC); and the opt-in Metal engine gives the largest factors, 47 and 37. The factors are therefore not a measure of the engine alone. The runs of cg31 and cg33 differ by 0.5\,s on each board
(2.4 against 1.9\,s, 9.2 against 8.7\,s): the two builds differ in the thread search described above, and no other cause was looked for.

\subsection*{The valley and the scheduling switches}
The allocation-placement valley of Section~\ref{sec:valley} is there on this chip under macOS~27.0, and the allocator's stagger
removes it: on the GCPW line (0.24\,M cells) cg33 runs 606\,\MC\ on one thread and 201 with \code{OPENEMS\_NO\_STAGGER=1}
(0.33$\times$), and 3\,167 and 1\,099 at 6 threads (0.35$\times$); stock runs 162 on one thread. Only the rates were measured. Of the
scheduling switches on notch x3 at 18 threads, the engine runs 0.88$\times$ as fast without the neighbour flags of cg29
(Section~\ref{sec:cg29}; \code{OPENEMS\_FLAGS=0}, 5\,534 against 6\,302\,\MC), and the default QoS class in place of
user-initiated (\code{OPENEMS\_QOS=0}) changes nothing measurable (1.005$\times$)
(\code{results/studio\_speed\_set\_2026-11-15.txt}, section 8).

\subsection*{What this set shows}
On this chip the CPU engine of cg33 is faster at 18 threads than at 6 or 12 on five of six models (the patch tutorial's best is 6
threads; the sweep of Table~\ref{tab:studiothreads} puts patch x5's best at 10) and
runs 3.08 to 16.3$\times$ stock's best, while stock peaks at 2 to 12 threads and slows down beyond; the Metal engine is 1.20 to
2.14$\times$ the CPU engine at 18 threads and leads most on the two large grids (1.86$\times$ and 2.14$\times$); the thread search
of patch 0041 costs 2 to 6\,\% of a short run on five models where cg31's rule cost 14 to 30\,\%; patch x5 peaks at 10 threads and the search
settles on 18, at 0.94 of that peak, for a cause not investigated; the allocation-placement valley exists under macOS~27.0 and the stagger removes
it; the neighbour flags still pay; and the tiled sweep pays only on a memory-bound grid with little extension work. Bit identity
held for every CPU run, as on the other two machines.
