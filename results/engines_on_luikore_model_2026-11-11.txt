Three builds on luikore's benchmark model, same Apple M5, interleaved, 2026-11-11 06:35-06:43 (quiet machine)
================================================================================================================
NOTE 2026-11-13 (conditions of the fork-against-master CPU ratio): "fork CPU against master cpu x1.27 and x0.86" below compares the
fork's multithreaded engine at the better of 8 and 10 threads with master's cpu engine at 8 threads only (master's was not run at 10).
In these runs the model file's endCriteria="0" also made both programs (not luikore's build) run the periodic field-energy estimate
described below, which was measured only for the Metal engine; its cost on the CPU engines was not measured. Master's cpu engine is a
different algorithm (one cache-sized tile shared by all threads, several timesteps per pass) and its probes differ from the fork's. A
paired comparison on the M5 Pro on 2026-11-13, with thread counts equal on both sides and a source on the mesh, is in
results/cpu_items_cg33_2026-11-13.txt (section 3 part c): the fork is ahead at 1 M cells and level to 14 % ahead at 8 M on the cases
without dispersion (x0.93 on pml at 5 threads), and behind on the 8 M dispersive case.

scripts/luikore_compare/compare3.py: luikore's Metal engine (openEMS pull request 228, commit 55ce11b), upstream master 1d710c2
(engines gpu, cpu, multithreaded) and the fork as installed that morning (cg31, v0.37.0-57-gff2538a = patches 0001 to 0039) on
luikore's own model (macos/bench/bench_metal.py: a uniform 1 mm grid with PEC walls, one dielectric box, a Gaussian point
excitation, no dumps; plus one voltage probe), every binary run from the same model file with its own library, three repetitions
per configuration, a run after the other processes fell to 1.5 cores or less. Other processes before the 48 runs: a median of
0.44 cores, at most 1.14. The other sessions on the machine were paused for this.

With the build of the night before (cg29, results/luikore_compare_cg29_2026-11-11.txt) the fork's Metal engine ran 3728 and 3092.
A model file with endCriteria="0" makes the openEMS program use its default of -60 dB (openems.cpp, ParseFDTDSetup), so in these
runs the fork and master estimate the energy every Nyquist period (51 steps here) during the first 60 s, one thread over both fields
while the engine waits, and luikore's build only with the progress report: about 5 % of the fork's loop. From Python, where
EndCriteria = 0 means none, the fork's Metal engine runs the same model at 4408 (3.0 M) and 3507 MC/s (17 M)
(results/metal_engine_cg31_2026-11-11.txt, final table).
The multithreaded engines of the three builds give identical probes; the Metal and gpu engines and master's cpu engine differ
from them by 7e-6 to 3e-5 relative (they flush subnormals, or reorder).
