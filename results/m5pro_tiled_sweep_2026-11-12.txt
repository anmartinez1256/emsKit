The tiled sweep (patch 0040, OPENEMS_TBLOCK) against the fused sweep on an Apple M5 Pro (Mac mini), 2026-11-12
====================================================================================================
Tiled = snapshot cg32d (the fork's commit 0ef186b = patch 0040) with OPENEMS_TBLOCK; fused = cg31 (ff2538a, patches 0001-0039).
What the tiled sweep is and its identity checks (also on this machine): results/tiled_sweep_cg32_2026-11-11.txt.
MACHINE: Mac mini, Apple M5 Pro (Mac17,16), 5 Super cores (hw.perflevel0, L2 16 MiB) + 10 Performance cores (two L2 of 8 MiB),
24 GB, macOS 27.2.1 (26B131). A set of its own: compared only with numbers from this machine (the M5 timing is in the record above).
When: 2026-11-12 03:36:18 to 04:16:43, one block of a night queue on the machine, scripts/m5pro/stageB.zsh (thread counts 5, 10, 15).
Method: bench/abtest.py, 3 interleaved rounds, medians in MC/s; gate: wait up to 20 s for the other processes to use at most
300 % CPU, redo a run once above that (scripts/m5pro/common.zsh). A first try at 02:23 with a stricter gate (70 %, 240 s) was
stopped: macOS's indexing kept the others at 1 to 3.5 cores, so nearly every run waited its full time (8 of 162 runs in 55 min).
Background: that indexing ran all night; the others' median per model is listed below, and every row's in the output per model.
On the PML cube the fused rows and the rows with OPENEMS_TBLOCK=4 and with OPENEMS_TBLOCK_HOOKS=0 ran beside 2.3 to 3.2 cores, the
row with OPENEMS_BALANCE=0 beside 1.5 and the tiled auto rows beside 0.15 to 0.74, so its x0.65 sets a loaded fused best against
a quiet tiled best. Other rows near or above one core: notch x3 tiled auto at 5 threads (1.14) and the 2.95 M box with
OPENEMS_TBLOCK=8, W=33 (3.25) and with OPENEMS_TBLOCK=4 (1.09).
Correctness: every one of the 162 runs gave probes identical to the reference run (cg31 at 5 threads).

Reading:
- The fused sweep scales to all 15 cores of this chip (x1.4 to x2.1 from 5 to 15 threads) except on the 16.8 M PEC box, where it
  is nearly flat (2986 to 3326): that grid is memory-bound, and the tiled sweep lifts it to 4752 (x1.43). This is the one model
  where the tiled sweep pays, the same model as in the M5 record.
- On the other five the tiled sweep loses (x0.41 to x0.97): the fused sweep keeps gaining from the ten Performance cores while the
  tiled sweep peaks at 10 threads and falls at 15. A reading, not measured: one barrier per task across all threads costs more
  as threads are added. The standalone kernel (scripts/kernel/kb_tile.cpp) gains x1.21 at 15 threads on this machine
  (results/m5pro_speed_set_2026-11-12.txt), so the engine loses something the kernel does not.
- Verdict for this chip, the same as on the M5: the tiled sweep stays opt-in, never a default, and nothing for the two boards.
  A rule that switches it on by itself would need the grid to be far past the cache and the fused sweep to be memory-bound, which
  only a timed comparison at run time can tell (not built).
