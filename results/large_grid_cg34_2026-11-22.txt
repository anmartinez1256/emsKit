The fork's CPU engine on large grids: y-banded fused sweep (cg34), final benchmark on the Apple M5 Max, 2026-11-22
====================================================================================================
MACHINE: Mac Studio (Mac17,14), Apple M5 Max, 6 "Super" cores (hw.perflevel0, one L2 of 16 MiB) + 12 "Performance" cores
(hw.perflevel1, two L2 of 8 MiB), 32-core GPU, 36 GB, macOS 27.0. A set of its own ("studio"); compared only with numbers of
this machine (results/studio_speed_set_2026-11-15.txt), never with the M5 MacBook Air or M5 Pro tables.

Why: with upstream's own benchmark/bench.py on this machine (2026-11-17, a source in every case) the fork's CPU engine (cg33,
13350ca) led upstream master's `cpu` engine (1d710c2) at 1 M cells (2.5 to 2.8 times on five cases) and at 8 M (1.10 to 1.29),
and was behind at 30 M: ideal 3755 against 4504 MC/s (0.83), pml / inhomogeneous / antenna 0.85 to 0.86, cylindrical 0.99,
dispersive 0.48. Goal of this work: lead at 30 M too, keep the 1 M and 8 M lead, CPU results bit-identical to v0.37.0.

Code: three commits on the fork's branch speed-m5-gpu on top of 13350ca (cg33); at the date of this record they are local
(not pushed, not installed, not yet in patches/):
  d918386  box hooks: extension hooks that take an x range and a y range (UPML, excitation, lumped RLC, steady-state detection);
           the opt-in tiled sweep runs them on each thread's own y lines                                        [snapshot cg34a]
  e05d419  y-banded fused sweep: every block of a thread's x-slab except the slab's last one is cut into bands of H y lines
           (voltages of the block, then currents of the lines behind, one row back in y), so the planes the sweep reuses stay
           in the thread's share of the L2 cache. OPENEMS_YBAND = 0 (off) | H (force) | auto (default when unset);
           OPENEMS_YBAND_CACHE = MiB a thread. auto: bands when four y-z planes of V and I exceed the thread's L2 share
           (hw.perflevelN.l2cachesize / cpusperl2, the smallest over the levels the threads occupy), H = half the share over
           four lines; all or nothing (an extension without box hooks keeps whole planes)                       [snapshot cg34b]
  8d513ba  box hooks for Mur (coupled rows never cut apart), conducting sheets / Lorentz / Drude / Debye (packed groups cut by
           the y range), closed cylinder (row 0 in the last band)                                               [snapshot cg34c]
On this machine auto gives: 311^3 lines 93-line bands at 6 threads, 46 at 12 and 18; 201^3 off at 6 threads, 71 at 12 and 18;
101^3 off.

Findings
  1. THE FORK LEADS ON FIVE OF SIX CASES AT 30 M CELLS. Best against best, the fork with y-bands runs 1.32 to 1.48 times the master of the
     2026-11-17 record (1d710c2) and 1.11 to 1.22 times upstream's head (ccb4472) on ideal, pml, inhomogeneous, antenna and
     cylindrical. Before the work the same comparison was 0.83 to 0.99 (2026-11-17); the cg33 column of this run gives 0.85 to 1.00
     against master and 0.74 to 0.79 against upstream's head.
  2. DISPERSIVE IS NOT MET (left for a pass of its own): 0.75 of master and 0.64 of upstream's head at 30 M, 0.75 / 0.66 at 8 M.
     What the y-bands do to it: 1.54 times the fork before at 30 M (1375 -> 2121; at 18 threads 1320 -> 2121 = 1.61), 1.09 at 8 M
     (at 18 threads 1600 -> 2068 = 1.29); the fork's best thread count moves from 6 (L) and 12 (M) to 18. Setup (13.3 s against 2.0 to
     2.2 s at L) and memory (6.6 GiB against 4.0 / 2.7) are as before.
  3. THE 1 M AND 8 M LEAD HOLDS. 1 M: bands stay off, cg34c = cg33 within 1 % on all six (0.99 to 1.01); the fork is 1.53 to 2.89 times
     master and 1.26 to 2.19 times upstream's head. 8 M: 1.09 to 1.28 times the fork before; 1.36 to 1.58 times master and 1.17 to 1.26
     times upstream's head on five cases (the fork before was only level with upstream's head there: 0.97 to 1.02).
  4. AT EQUAL THREAD COUNTS the gain is at 12 and 18 threads (30 M: 1.45 to 1.61 times cg33). At 6 threads (the Super cores, not held
     back by the bus) bands change little: 0.96 to 1.03 of cg33 at 30 M, and bands are off at 8 M. There the old master leads the fork
     on the cases with extension work: pml / inhomogeneous / antenna 0.94 to 0.96 at 30 M and 0.85 to 0.89 at 8 M, dispersive 0.71 to
     0.73; upstream's head is level or behind at 6 threads (1.01 to 1.27 for the fork, dispersive 0.71 / 0.90).
     One point below its reference at 8 M: antenna at 12 threads 2854 against cg33's 2916 [2539-2947] (0.98, inside that spread).
  5. BIT-IDENTITY. cg34c against cg33: probes identical on 54 of 54 rows (six cases x three sizes x three thread counts; bands on in 30 of
     them, including the closed cylinder with row 0 in the last band). cg33 is identical to stock v0.37.0 (earlier records).
     Both upstream cpu engines differ from the fork: 1d710c2 by 1.3e-7 to 2.9e-5 (relative); ccb4472 by 1.1e-7 to 1.8e-5 on five cases
     and by 7.2e-4 (L), 8.8e-4 (M) and 1.4e-2 to 2.1e-2 (S) on cylindrical, beyond bench.py's tolerance ("MISMATCH"); its gpu engine
     differs from 1d710c2's gpu engine on cylindrical by the same amounts (7.2e-4, 8.8e-4, 2.8e-2). Which side is right on the
     cylindrical case was not looked into here; the fork agrees with v0.37.0 and with 1d710c2 to 1e-5.
  6. Reference column: upstream's gpu engines are the fastest column at 30 M and 8 M on pml, inhomogeneous, antenna and dispersive
     (ccb4472: 3802 to 5120 MC/s at L); the fork's CPU engine is level with them on ideal and cylindrical at L (5719 / 5797 against
     4786-5944 / 4770-5601) and ahead at S on ideal and cylindrical.
  7. Upstream's head uses about a third to a half of the memory of the others (ideal L 976 MiB against 2816 for the fork).
